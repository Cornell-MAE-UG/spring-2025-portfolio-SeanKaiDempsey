Rather than relying on a physical test bench, this project focused entirely on the mathematical modeling and control simulation of a thermal system. The objective was to design a PID controller capable of regulating a coffee warmer's temperature while minimizing steady-state error and settling time.

### Mathematical Modeling & ODE Formulation
I derived the governing differential equations for the system by establishing a transient thermal energy balance. The model accounts for reference temperature input, conductive heat transfer through the heating element, and convective/radiative losses to the environment.

$$\rho V C_p \frac{dT}{dt} = q_{in} - h A (T - T_{\infty}) - \epsilon \sigma A (T^4 - T_{\infty}^4)$$

Using MATLAB, my group set up and solved these non-linear ordinary differential equations (ODEs) to simulate the open-loop plant response and characterize the system's inherent thermal lag.

### Simulation-Driven PID Tuning
With the plant dynamics characterized, I implemented a feedback control loop. Because a physical prototype was not available, I used a systematic **trial-and-error tuning methodology within the simulation environment**:
- **Proportional Gain:** Increased to accelerate the initial rise time, balancing against system overshoot.
- **Integral Gain:** Introduced and tuned to eliminate the steady-state error inherent to the convective cooling losses.
- **Derivative Gain:** Adjusted cautiously to add damping and smooth out the transient approach to the target temperature.

The final optimized controller achieved a stable response that safely balanced fast thermal response times against crazy high control input.
