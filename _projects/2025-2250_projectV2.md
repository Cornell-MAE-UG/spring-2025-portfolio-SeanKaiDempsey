MAE 2250's Original Design Project challenged a team of five to identify a real problem, develop a solution through iterative prototyping, and think through how it would actually be manufactured and brought to market. Our team, CHASR Solutions, designed the **Candom**: a universal spill-proof cap for open canned beverages.

![Candom Final CAD Render]({{ "/assets/images/CapOnCan.jpg" | relative_url }}){: style="width: 150px; display: block; margin: 0 auto;"}


The problem we focused on was simple: college students frequently crack open a caffeinated canned drink, don't finish it, and then have an open can sitting on their desk ready to spill. No good solution existed that was universal across can sizes, resealable, and actually portable.

![Candom Final CAD Render]({{ "/assets/images/2250-CanCap-Render.png" | relative_url }}){: style="width: 500px; display: block; margin: 0 auto;"}

## My Role

I led the CAD development throughout the project by translating Hugo's sketches into Fusion 360 models across all three prototype iterations and the final design. Beyond CAD, I contributed significantly to the Design for Manufacturing (DFM) and Design for Assembly (DFA) analyses, helped develop the user manual and supplementary materials, and drove decisions around ergonomics, sustainability, and overall design intent.

## The Design

The Candom has three main components: a **primary body** that clamps over the top of the can, a **locking nut** that threads onto the primary to tighten it universally across different can diameters using tapered threads, and a **rotating lid** that spins to seal or expose the drinking hole.

![Candom Engineering Drawing]({{ "/assets/images/2250-CanCap-Drawing.png" | relative_url }}){: style="width: 500px; display: block; margin: 0 auto;"}

The rotating lid was 3D printed in TPU for its rubber-like flexibility and food-safe properties, which helped it conform and seal. An O-ring on the primary body provided a secondary seal between the cap and the can body. The locking nut used tapered threads to accommodate slight variation in can diameter. The key feature that made universality possible.

![Candom 3D Printed Model with O-ring]({{ "/assets/images/2250-CanCap-Model.png" | relative_url }}){: style="width: 500px; display: block; margin: 0 auto;"}

## Prototyping & Iteration

We went through three printed prototypes before arriving at the final design, each one revealing new issues:

![Candom Final CAD Render]({{ "/assets/images/CanPrototypes.jpg" | relative_url }}){: style="width: 500px; display: block; margin: 0 auto;"}

**Prototype 1** established the basic form: a primary body, locking nut, and rotating lid. It confirmed the concept was physically viable but revealed sizing and thread engagement problems.

**Prototype 2** refined the lid geometry and thread profile. The tab lid was redesigned and the locking nut threads were tightened up.

**Prototype 3** focused on the locking nut alone, iterating on the tapered thread geometry which was the most critical and most difficult feature to get right.

**Final Prototype** added the O-ring seal, ergonomic grip texture on the locking nut, and a snap-fit lid mechanism; replacing the original screw retention which caused leakage and assembly complexity.

## Design for Manufacturing

I contributed to the DFM analysis which evaluated how each component would be mass-produced. The primary body and locking nut were specified for **injection molding** due to their threaded geometry requiring tight tolerances. The rotating lid was specified for **vacuum forming** using movable mold sections to handle the undercut geometry, which is a cheaper tooling approach than injection molding while preserving the design.

![Candom Final CAD Render]({{ "/assets/images/CanCapDFA.png" | relative_url }}){: style="width: 50%; display: block; margin: 0 auto;"}

We also designed custom **assembly fixtures**: one to hold the locking nut while the primary is threaded on, and another to hold the primary steady while the lid is snapped into place. The fixtures where designed around existing geometry on the parts to avoid adding features purely for fixturing.

## Sustainability

The primary body and locking nut were specified in **HDPE** which is highly recyclable, injection moldable with excellent flow properties, and corrosion resistant for a 10+ year product lifecycle. The lid used **TPU** for its sealing properties, and the O-ring used nitrile rubber. We analyzed the full carbon footprint at **0.57 kg CO₂e per unit**, with assembly being the largest contributor at 58%. We identified that switching manufacturing from China to a domestic facility in Houston (near HDPE sourcing in La Porte, TX) could reduce the total footprint by 35% to **0.37 kg CO₂e**.

## Results & Reflection

The Candom prototype did not achieve a reliable seal. The tapered thread mechanism, the feature that made universality possible, was also the feature that made sealing difficult. Getting threads tight enough to seal against the can without requiring excessive torque proved harder than anticipated, and the interaction between the tapered threads and the O-ring was difficult to control across different can geometries.

Our team's final recommendation was to discontinue the Candom. An honest market analysis found that the niche is small, competition from silicone can toppers is already established at lower price points, and the manufacturing complexity of getting the tapered threads right at scale would be a significant cost barrier.

That conclusion is one of the most valuable things that came out of the project. Knowing when a concept has fundamental limitations, and being able to articulate why, is just as important an engineering skill as making something work. I learned a lot about how early design decisions (like committing to tapered threads for universality) create downstream constraints that compound through manufacturing, assembly, and sealing performance. If I were to continue the project, I'd explore a different universality mechanism such as a compliant O-ring-only clamping system that doesn't require precision threaded engagement to seal.
